# Premium visual and motion pass — the owner's brief

*Received 2026-11-26 15:07. Decisions: Butter (light) + Cast Iron (dark) merged into one system, each person picks Light / Dark / Match my phone; pinboard week with tonight's dinner on top; build after the first launch; 15:23 "Fix all the issues you have in the audit" — every screen.*

Give Basket a premium visual and motion pass, the kind of finish a top visual designer and motion designer would put on it. Keep every feature, all data, and the tests working. This pass is about craft, not new features.

DIRECTION (already decided, don't reopen): A favorite cookbook crossed with a well-kept kitchen. Food photos are the star. No SaaS look. Mood references: Half Baked Harvest (rich, abundant, cozy food photography), Pinterest and Instagram (image-led, effortless to browse). Take the mood, never the layouts or photos.

WHAT PREMIUM MEANS HERE
- Type: one or two typefaces with real character and a strict scale of five or six sizes. Tune letter and line spacing per size. Real fractions (½ cup), curly quotes, and aligned number columns for prices.
- Spacing: one spacing scale used everywhere. Align to what the eye sees, not to box edges. More breathing room than feels necessary.
- Color: a small palette drawn from food and kitchen materials. Neutrals tinted toward the palette, never plain grey. One accent, used sparingly. Dark mode tuned on its own, not inverted.
- Light and depth: one light source. Soft layered shadows tinted by the surface beneath, used only to show what sits on top.
- Photos: consistent crops and one subtle shared color treatment so recipe photos from different sites look like one set. Kroger product shots sit on a matching tile so white-background packshots don't look pasted in. Photos load from a blurred color preview, and a missing or low-quality photo gets a designed fallback. Sample dishes are vegetarian.
- Icons and corners: one icon set with the same line weight throughout. Corner radius scales with element size.
- Every state designed: pressed, loading, empty, error, offline, very long names, over budget.
- Edges: correct spacing around the notch and gesture bar on iPhone 17 and common Android phones, matching status bar color, app icon and splash screen in the same style.

MOTION
- Define a motion language first: two or three durations and one or two spring curves, used everywhere.
- Touch: everything tappable responds instantly with a small press effect.
- Continuity: a recipe photo grows from its card into the recipe page and shrinks back. Sheets follow the finger and can be flicked away. Reordered items slide aside for each other.
- Three signature moments, done beautifully: a meal settling into its day with a soft spring; a shopping item's strike-through drawing across as it eases down the list; the budget total rolling to its new number when the cart changes.
- Opening the week: one gentle staggered reveal, on open only, never on scroll.
- Rules: smooth on a mid-range Android, never blocks a tap, nothing over about 400ms except signature moments, gentle fades when reduce-motion is on, a light haptic tick where supported. Prefer what the browser and current code offer over a heavy animation library.

HOW TO WORK
1. Screenshot every screen at iPhone 17 and a common Android size, light and dark. List the ten things that most make it look unfinished.
2. Write the design values (color, type, spacing, corners, shadows, motion) in one place so every screen draws from them.
3. Apply it all to the weekly plan screen first. Show before/after screenshots and a short recording of each animation.
4. Before showing me anything, check it at 200% zoom for misalignment and uneven spacing, check text contrast, and remove one decorative thing.
5. After I approve, roll out to the remaining screens with before/after screenshots of each.
